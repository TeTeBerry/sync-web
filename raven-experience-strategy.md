# Raven Experience Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines how Raven balances immersive brand experience and product usability across different pages.

Raven is not a single type of experience.

Different pages have different responsibilities.

Some pages should create emotion.

Some pages should help users make decisions.

Some pages should help users complete actions.

The experience balance changes depending on the user's journey stage.

---

# Core Principle

Every page must feel like Raven.

However, not every page should have the same level of immersion.

The goal is not:

"Make every page cinematic."

The goal is:

"Create the right balance between emotion and utility at every moment."

---

# Experience Scale

## Brand Experience

Measures:

- emotional connection
- visual immersion
- storytelling
- anticipation
- memorability

High brand experience means:

The user feels something.

---

## Product Utility

Measures:

- clarity
- efficiency
- decision making
- information access
- task completion

High product utility means:

The user can achieve their goal quickly.

---

# Page Experience Distribution


# Homepage

## Role

Brand Entrance.

The homepage introduces the Raven world.

It is the user's first emotional interaction with Raven.

The goal is not to explain every feature.

The goal is to create curiosity and desire.

---

## Experience Balance

Brand Experience:

80%

Product Utility:

20%

---

## Experience Priorities

Emotion:

90%

Storytelling:

90%

Visual Atmosphere:

90%

Information:

30%

Conversion:

40%

---

## User Feeling

The user should think:

"I want to explore this."

"I want to experience this."

"Raven feels different."

---

## Design Direction

The homepage should prioritize:

- immersive hero
- strong visual identity
- anticipation
- emotional storytelling
- premium brand feeling

The homepage should not prioritize:

- dense feature explanation
- detailed data
- complex navigation
- comparison

---

# Events Page

## Role

Festival Discovery.

Events helps users discover their next festival.

It is not a database.

It is the exploration layer of Raven.

---

## Experience Balance

Brand Experience:

45%

Product Utility:

55%

---

## Experience Priorities

Discovery:

90%

Comparison:

70%

Emotion:

60%

Information:

70%

Utility:

70%

---

## User Feeling

The user should think:

"Where should I go next?"

"Which festival matches me?"

"I want to explore more."

---

## Design Direction

The Events page should balance:

- immersive festival presentation
- efficient browsing
- useful information
- festival comparison

The page should feel more emotional than a traditional event listing.

The page should feel more useful than a marketing landing page.

---

## Avoid

Do not create:

- generic event grids
- database feeling
- marketplace feeling
- filter-heavy interfaces

The user is exploring possibilities, not managing records.

---

# Event Detail Page

## Role

Festival Experience.

This is where curiosity becomes desire.

The page should make the user emotionally connect with a festival.

---

## Experience Balance

Brand Experience:

60%

Product Utility:

40%

---

## Experience Priorities

Emotion:

90%

Storytelling:

90%

Festival Identity:

90%

Information:

70%

Decision Support:

60%

---

## User Feeling

The user should think:

"I want to be there."

"This festival matches my taste."

"I can imagine myself there."

---

## Design Direction

The Event Detail page should communicate:

- what this festival feels like
- why this lineup matters
- why this trip is worth taking
- how Raven can help make it happen

The festival is the hero.

The information supports the emotion.

---

## Content Priority

The experience order should follow:

Festival feeling

↓

Lineup discovery

↓

Personal relevance

↓

Travel confidence

↓

Trip planning

---

## Avoid

Do not make the page feel like:

- Wikipedia
- ticket marketplace
- event database
- information archive

---

The user already wants to go.

65%

---

## Experience Priorities

Decision Making:

90%

Clarity:

90%

Journey Understanding:

80%

Emotion:

90%

---

## User Feeling

The user should think:

"This trip is possible."

"Raven has prepared everything."

"I know what to do next."

---

## Design Direction

The Plan page should prioritize:

- clear journey flow
- travel confidence
- useful recommendations

# Final Evaluation

Before designing any page, evaluate:

1. What is this page's responsibility?

2. Should the user feel something or complete something?

3. What is the correct balance between emotion and utility?

4. Does this page continue the Raven experience?

5. Does this page respect its assigned experience ratio?

A successful Raven page is not the most beautiful page.

It is the page with the correct balance between emotion and usefulness.
